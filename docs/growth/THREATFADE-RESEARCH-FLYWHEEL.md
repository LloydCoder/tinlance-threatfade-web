# ThreatFade Research Flywheel

**Status:** Strategic baseline  
**Date:** 2026-08-25  
**Scope:** Detection science, experiments, benchmarks, publications, and research-to-product feedback  

## Purpose

Make original ThreatFade research the upstream engine for product differentiation, authoritative content, technical trust, OSS adoption, and commercial evidence.

## Flywheel

```text
Threat / detection question
        ↓
Hypothesis
        ↓
Controlled experiment
        ↓
ThreatFade analysis
        ↓
Evidence + metrics + limitations
        ↓
Benchmark / research artifact
        ↓
Product + detection-pack improvement
        ↓
Publication / content / GitHub
        ↓
Researchers + practitioners + customers
        ↓
New questions and datasets
        ↺
```

## Research priorities

1. Fade-event characterization.
2. Encrypted-traffic behavioral detection.
3. Rolling Shannon entropy and temporal features.
4. Multi-domain signal fusion.
5. C2 quieting/evasive beaconing.
6. Living-off-the-land network behavior.
7. False-positive and false-negative characterization.
8. Detection-pack benchmarking.
9. Adversarial robustness.
10. Reproducibility and deployment methodology.

Future research themes must be promoted only after the underlying hypothesis is technically justified.

## Research artifact standard

Every publishable study should document:

- question;
- hypothesis;
- environment;
- data source;
- methodology;
- parameters;
- baseline;
- observed result;
- uncertainty;
- limitations;
- reproducibility path;
- ThreatFade version;
- conclusion;
- follow-up questions.

## Research-to-product loop

A useful finding should create one or more of:

- detection-pack improvement;
- benchmark;
- regression test;
- evidence format improvement;
- analyst workflow improvement;
- documentation;
- integration requirement;
- roadmap item.

Research that cannot improve knowledge, detection, validation, or product direction should not automatically receive priority.

## Research-to-content loop

One validated research project can generate:

- canonical research note;
- technical article;
- evidence visualization;
- reproducible example;
- GitHub artifact;
- documentation;
- conference submission;
- short-form technical content;
- newsletter item;
- sales evidence where appropriate.

The research artifact remains the source of truth.

## Research-to-commercial loop

Enterprise prospects should be shown measured evidence where applicable, not generic claims.

A research finding can support:

**Research → benchmark → assessment methodology → pilot hypothesis → customer evaluation.**

Customer work must not be presented as independent research unless the methodology and provenance justify that claim.

## Metrics

Track:

- experiments completed;
- validated findings;
- benchmark coverage;
- regression coverage;
- research citations/references;
- qualified product activations attributed to research;
- GitHub adoption attributed to research;
- assessment/evaluation opportunities influenced by research;
- product improvements generated by research.

## Quality gates

No publication should claim more than the evidence supports. Distinguish measured findings, interpretation, hypotheses, and roadmap concepts. Document known limitations and avoid implying that an anomaly alone proves compromise.

## Definition of a healthy research flywheel

Research continuously produces defensible technical knowledge; that knowledge improves ThreatFade; improved ThreatFade produces stronger evidence; evidence attracts users and customers; new users and customer-safe findings generate better research questions.

## Relationship to other flywheels

**Research → Content → Demand → Conversion → Commercial → Customer Value → Product/OSS → Research.**
